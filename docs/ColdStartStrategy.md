# SchoolWise Cold-Start Strategy

**Goal:** Acquire the first 100 approved reviews from distinct, verified parents in Greece.  
**Scope:** Go-to-market strategy for the partner-school Phase 2 pilot; no code or marketing automation is proposed here.

## Executive recommendation

Start with **one dense local cluster**—Athens/Attica or Thessaloniki, chosen by the founders' strongest school and parent-association introductions—not a nationwide launch. Recruit 5–10 schools, arrange private per-parent delivery of individual one-time verification codes through each school's own channel, and invite the full eligible parent community rather than asking schools to nominate “happy” parents. Never put a redeemable code in a public QR code, general newsletter, or shared class password.

Aim for **100 published reviews from 100 distinct verified parents across about 10 schools** (roughly 10 per school). This provides enough reviews for the proposed five-review score threshold while producing a more useful school-choice test than 100 reviews concentrated on one listing. If only five schools are ready, target at least 20 reviews each and be transparent about the limited sample.

Do not pay for reviews, reward positive ratings, buy parent contact lists, or ask schools to transfer parent data. The school or parent association distributes the invitation; each parent chooses whether to visit, redeem a code, and publish a review. Verification and moderation rules stay independent of partnerships. A code is evidence that the account redeemed a token distributed through an agreed school channel; it is not government-ID or legal-identity verification. The badge and privacy notice must explain this accurately.

## 1. Define the pilot and success measures

### Cohort

- Choose one metro area based on warm access and school density. Do not divide early effort across all of Greece.
- Recruit 5 schools for the first learning wave, then expand toward 10 if the first schools distribute codes and parents complete reviews.
- Seek a mix of public/private schools and kindergarten/elementary coverage where partner readiness permits. Do not claim national or representative coverage from a small convenience sample.
- Use only schools with an authorized point of contact and a clear way to reach currently or recently enrolled parents.
- State that pilot participation is not a SchoolWise endorsement of the school and gives the school no review-control rights.

### North-star and guardrails

**North-star:** Published reviews from distinct verified parents, distributed across participating schools.

Track weekly:

1. Number of schools that accepted the participation rules and actually distributed invitations.
2. Invitations/codes distributed by school and channel (aggregate counts only).
3. Landing-page visits → email-confirmed accounts → code redemptions → review submissions → approved/published reviews.
4. Unique reviewers and reviews per school; share of schools with at least five distinct published reviewers.
5. Median moderation time, rejection/appeal outcomes, report rate, and privacy/safety incidents.
6. Parent actions after reading: school shortlisting, profile comparison, contact/open-day clicks, and qualitative feedback.
7. Partner willingness to distribute another cohort and parent willingness to recommend SchoolWise.

Do not optimize for a higher school score, a high approval rate, or raw sign-ups. A low score or a rejection is not a campaign failure if the process is fair and transparent.

### Planning funnel (hypothesis, not a Greek-market benchmark)

To reach 100 published reviews, plan for approximately **800 school-distributed invitations**:

| Funnel step | Planning assumption | Approximate volume |
|---|---:|---:|
| Invitations delivered by participating schools | — | 800 |
| Parent visits the pilot page | 25% | 200 |
| Parent creates/confirm account and redeems an eligible code | 80% of visitors | 160 |
| Parent submits a review | 80% of verified parents | 128 |
| Submitted review passes neutral policy moderation | 85% of submissions | 109 |

These are only capacity-planning assumptions. Measure the first 100–150 invitations across two schools and replace them with actual funnel rates before asking more schools to distribute codes. Do not increase invitations by weakening proof or review rules.

## 2. School and listing data acquisition

### Build a reliable catalogue before soliciting reviews

An empty or inaccurate catalogue undermines trust. Seed factual school profiles independently of review collection:

- Use the Ministry of Education's public primary- and secondary-school search pages as starting points for public school names, type, and location. Record the source and last-checked date; verify current operation and canonical name against the school's own official page before publishing.
- Use EETAA's ChildCare24/program information and provider lists as **lead sources** for participating childcare structures. EETAA describes relevant lists as indicative/non-exhaustive and tied to a program year; do not treat them as a complete licensing register or proof that a provider is currently operating.
- For private schools, seek introductions through the Hellenic Association of Independent Schools / Σύνδεσμος Ιδιωτικών Σχολείων (SIS), then confirm details with each school. An association introduction is not an endorsement or an automatic right to publish its members' information.
- For nurseries/kindergartens, confirm the applicable municipality/authority and current provider information directly; do not infer licensing status from a promotional listing.
- Add only useful, factual fields that parents need to compare. Attribute or link to public official sources where appropriate and provide a correction/contact path.

Do not copy school photos, marketing copy, reviews, or logos from third-party directories without checking usage rights. Do not scrape and republish Google/social-media reviews as SchoolWise reviews. Never create placeholder ratings, invented reviews, or fabricated “verified” counts to make profiles look active.

### Keep school data separate from parent data

- Store catalogue source/verification metadata; do not use directory data to build parent contact lists.
- Do not buy, scrape, infer, or accept a spreadsheet of family names, emails, phone numbers, student names, or class rosters.
- A school or parent association should distribute SchoolWise's invitation through its own lawful channel and privately deliver one unique code per eligible parent. SchoolWise receives only aggregate distribution counts unless an individual parent independently signs up.
- School profile corrections may be accepted from the school, but partnerships must not allow the school to identify reviewers, suppress criticism, edit reviews, or influence ratings.

## 3. School onboarding and partnership offer

### Ideal first partners

Prioritize schools or parent associations that:

- already communicate digitally with parents;
- can distribute the same invitation to all eligible families, not a management-selected group;
- accept one-time code distribution without sending SchoolWise a parent roster;
- agree that participation is voluntary and never conditional on a positive review;
- accept SchoolWise's independent moderation and no-veto policy; and
- can nominate one contact for school facts, code logistics, and correction requests.

Use the school's existing parent channel rather than trying to acquire its audience directly. For a public school, start with the parent association and follow the school/municipal approval process required locally. Do not imply that a principal, municipality, Ministry, or association endorses SchoolWise unless it has expressly agreed to that wording.

### Partner value exchange

Offer:

- a free factual profile review/correction during the pilot;
- a clearly labeled “pilot school” information page, without a paid or higher-trust badge;
- aggregate, privacy-protected feedback only when the minimum cohort threshold is met; and
- a short post-pilot findings session on parents' information needs.

Do not offer better ratings, favorable review visibility, access to parent identities, privileged moderation, removal on demand, or compensation tied to review count/sentiment. Do not sell placement during the initial trust pilot.

### School onboarding checklist

1. Confirm the school's correct legal/public name, type, location, and approved public contact.
2. Explain the verified-parent definition, one-time code rules, review policy, privacy notice, and deletion/report process.
3. Agree in writing that the invitation goes to the whole eligible cohort (or a neutral random sample), not only known supporters.
4. Generate unique single-use codes for that school and define expiry, handoff, revocation, and lost-code support. Deliver a different code privately to each intended parent; do not share one code with a class or the public.
5. Agree on a distribution channel controlled by the school/association; SchoolWise does not receive a mailing list. The school/association confirms it has permission/a lawful basis to use that channel for an external pilot invitation; do not assume its existing relationship automatically permits promotion.
6. Train the contact to answer parent questions without collecting reviews or forwarding review content to school staff.
7. Publish the school profile only after factual review; clearly say the school cannot see who reviewed it or change a rating.
8. Review aggregate participation and operational issues after the first two weeks; never share small-group or author-level data.

If a school will only distribute codes to parents it expects to write positive reviews, do not onboard it.

## 4. Parent acquisition channels

### Priority order

| Channel | Role in first 100 | Practical execution | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Partner school newsletters/parent portals** | Primary verified-parent reach | School sends one Greek-first invitation and one neutral reminder through a lawful, private per-parent channel; each parent receives an individual code | No parent list transfer; invite all eligible families; no review/score condition; never share a common redeemable code |
| **Σύλλογοι Γονέων & Κηδεμόνων and local parent federations** | Trusted distribution and feedback | Ask elected representatives to share an opt-in pilot link at meetings/newsletters; offer a short demo and Q&A | Do not collect membership rosters or present an association as an endorser without permission |
| **Parent-to-parent sharing** | Organic reach after the first reviews | Add a simple share link; referrals still need their own school code and eligibility | No reward for a review, positive rating, or code redemption; no platform-sent messages to a referrer's contact list |
| **Local parent communities** | Supplemental reach | Obtain group-admin approval before posting in Greek parenting groups, municipality/community pages, and school-community forums | Follow group rules; no scraping, unsolicited DMs, repeated posts, or review solicitation targeted by a child's data |
| **School open days and parent events** | School partner leads and product research | Ask organizers for permission to offer a QR code to parents; collect opt-in interest and interviews | Many open-day visitors are prospective parents, not eligible reviewers; do not count them as verified supply |
| **Greek-language organic search** | Long-term parent discovery | Publish accurate location/type pages and helpful explainers; use honest language about where reviews exist and where they do not | Do not create SEO pages with fake scores, thin duplicate content, or misleading “best school” claims |
| **Local education/parenting media** | Awareness and credibility | Offer a founder interview or neutral guide to comparing schools; disclose any paid/sponsored placement | No outlet receives a favorable review quota or editorial control over ratings |
| **Paid ads** | Only after channel evidence | Small, geographically limited search test after the partner funnel works | Avoid targeting minors or sensitive child/education profiles; stop if cost per verified parent is worse than school distribution |

### Greece-specific messaging

- Use Greek-first invitations, support, review rules, and status messages. Add English where the pilot includes international/bilingual school communities, not as a substitute for Greek moderation.
- Start with one local cluster where parents commonly compare schools and founders can obtain warm introductions. Add Thessaloniki or other regions only after the first cohort validates the code, review, and moderation process.
- Make the offer about helping families compare practical information and first-hand experience—not “rating teachers,” “exposing bad schools,” or guaranteeing school quality.
- Explain the small-sample state: a profile may have no score until enough distinct verified parents have reviewed it.
- Invite parents based on their relationship to the school, not on household income, a child's academic/health status, or their predicted sentiment.

## 5. Incentives and review integrity

### Do not incentivize a review

For the first 100:

- No gift card, discount, raffle, tuition benefit, school merchandise, or referral reward in exchange for submitting any review.
- Never condition an incentive on a positive rating, public publication, review length, or a particular school.
- Do not ask schools to give benefits for reviews or to share only positive reviews.
- Do not suppress low/negative reviews to preserve partner relationships.
- Do not advertise a paid “verified” status or boost a school's score.

This keeps the reviewer's decision independent and avoids a perception that ratings are bought. EU consumer rules address fake/commissioned and manipulated reviews in relevant trader contexts; counsel should confirm the exact application to SchoolWise and participating schools. SchoolWise should adopt transparent verification and anti-manipulation practices regardless.

### Acceptable research compensation

If parents are invited to a separate usability interview or product-research session, a modest fixed thank-you payment may be offered for the time, **regardless of whether they write, publish, or like a review**. Keep the research consent and compensation separate from the review flow, disclose the arrangement, collect no unnecessary child information, and ask Greek counsel/accounting to approve payment mechanics.

The partner value exchange should be free factual profile correction and aggregate learning after privacy thresholds—not a rating benefit.

## 6. Referral program

Use a **no-reward referral loop** in the MVP:

1. After a parent completes the review flow, show a share button with neutral wording such as “Know another parent at this school? Share SchoolWise.”
2. The share link identifies only the acquisition source/campaign, not the referring parent's review or identity.
3. The recipient must independently create an account, confirm email, and redeem their own single-use school code.
4. Do not give referrers points, cash, discounts, or leaderboard status for reviews or positive ratings.
5. Allow opt-out and do not have SchoolWise upload a phone book, send invitations to entered third-party email addresses, or reveal who referred whom to the school.

If referrals later become material, test non-monetary research/event invitations before considering rewards. Any benefit connected with a review must be reviewed for disclosure and consumer-law implications and is not recommended.

## 7. First-100 campaign plan

### Preparation (before the public pilot)

- Build a list of 30 candidate schools from official directories and warm founder/parent-community introductions; qualify 10.
- Confirm five schools first, with a path to ten; seek variety without claiming statistical representativeness.
- Prepare accurate profiles, a Greek landing page, school partner FAQ, privacy notice, review rules, invitation copy, and a short code FAQ.
- Agree on code quantities and distribution dates. SchoolWise generates unique codes; the school privately delivers one code to each eligible parent without transferring contact data.
- Recruit and train the moderator/operator before inviting parents. Set daily queue capacity and an urgent privacy/safety contact.
- Instrument aggregate funnel events with short campaign IDs (school/cohort/channel), not names, emails, or child details.

### Wave 1: prove distribution and flow

- Start with two schools and approximately 100–150 invitations/codes in total.
- Ask the school/association to share the invitation with the whole agreed eligible group.
- Hold a short parent Q&A or office hour; answer questions about privacy, anonymity limits, verification, and the school's lack of control.
- Review every code redemption and review through the intended product workflow. Interview a small number of completers and abandoners, with separate consent.
- Fix confusing instructions or broken steps before scaling. Do not change review rules to improve conversion.

### Wave 2: scale across the pilot

- Expand to 5–10 schools and enough delivered invitations to test the planning funnel (initially around 800 total, adjusted to actual wave-1 conversion).
- Use at most one neutral reminder through each partner's own channel; stop if the school reports parent fatigue or a complaint.
- Check distribution by school so the most enthusiastic partner does not dominate the sample.
- Publish approved reviews promptly and calculate ratings only from eligible published reviews; never seed the score with staff, founders, or unverified testimonials.
- Reach 100 distinct published reviews; aim for around 10 per school and at least five reviewers per school before displaying a numeric score.

### Weekly operating cadence

- **Monday:** review funnel, code inventory/expiry, school coverage, and queue backlog.
- **Midweek:** moderation calibration and one partner check-in; clear urgent privacy reports first.
- **Friday:** review acquisition conversion, rejection/appeal reasons, school balance, parent feedback, and next-week invitation cap.
- Pause a school's campaign if it selects only favorable reviewers, pressures parents, asks for identities, or fails to distribute corrections/complaints fairly.

## 8. Short school invitation template (Greek)

> **Θέμα: Πρόσκληση συμμετοχής σε πιλοτικό πρόγραμμα του SchoolWise**
>
> Το SchoolWise βοηθά οικογένειες να συγκρίνουν σχολεία με σαφείς πληροφορίες και εμπειρίες γονέων. Θα θέλαμε να προσκαλέσουμε προαιρετικά τις οικογένειες της σχολικής κοινότητας να συμμετάσχουν στο πιλοτικό πρόγραμμα, με μοναδικό κωδικό επαλήθευσης που διανέμεται από το σχολείο.
>
> Η συμμετοχή είναι εθελοντική. Δεν ζητάμε από το σχολείο κατάλογο γονέων και δεν κοινοποιούμε στο σχολείο ποιος υπέβαλε αξιολόγηση. Το σχολείο δεν εγκρίνει, τροποποιεί ή αφαιρεί αξιολογήσεις και η πρόσκληση δεν συνεπάγεται θετική αξιολόγηση ή endorsement από το SchoolWise. Πριν από τη διανομή, θα θέλαμε να επιβεβαιώσουμε μαζί σας το κείμενο ενημέρωσης, τη διαδικασία κωδικών και τα στοιχεία του δημόσιου προφίλ.

Have Greek counsel/privacy lead approve the final invitation, consent and code wording before distribution. The template is not a legal notice.

## 9. Capacity, risks, and stop conditions

The first 100 reviews are primarily a **distribution and human-operations challenge**, not an infrastructure challenge. At 3–5 minutes per review, direct moderation alone may take 5–9 hours for 100 submissions; add support, appeals, calibration, partner onboarding, and interviews. Reserve 15–25 staff hours as a planning estimate and keep a named moderator/operator separate from the single developer where possible.

Pause intake rather than weaken controls if:

- parents are not sure what “verified” means or believe their school can see their review;
- codes are being shared publicly or redeemed in suspicious bursts;
- partner schools selectively solicit only satisfied families;
- the moderation queue misses its agreed service target or lacks Greek-language coverage;
- a parent reports exposure of a child/staff identity, coercion, or retaliation;
- legal/privacy questions about school code distribution or review publication remain unresolved; or
- a school asks for reviewer identities, favorable treatment, or review removal without policy/legal grounds.

The first 100 are a learning cohort, not proof of nationwide product-market fit. The next decision is whether parents trust the process, take useful school-choice actions, and whether partner channels can repeat distribution without compromising independence.
